Body -> superior vena cava from upper body, inferior vena cava from lower body -> right atrium -> right ventricle -> pulmonary artery -> lungs -> pulmonary veins -> left atrium -> left ventricle -> aorta -> body

Aorta goes up and over your heart

Tricuspid valve in the right side, bicuspid on the left hand side

Coronary arteries are the first branches of the aorta

Pericardium - protective fluid filled sac surrounding the heart
Layers -
* Fibrous - connects the sac to the chest wall
* Parietal serous - connects to the inside of the fibrous
* Visceral - closest to the heart

Veins in the thorax:
Where subclavian and jugular join is the brachiocephalic
Azygos right
Hemiazygos left

Vessels of the abdomen:
Superior mesenteric artery supplied first part of the large intestine
Inferior mesenteric artery supplies second part of the large intestine

Aorta branches off at L4 into common iliac arteries

Arterial supply to the Upper Limb:
Subclavian artery -> axillary artery -> brachial artery -> splits into radial and ulnar artery 
Taking someone's pulse happens with the radial artery

When the body is in the anatomical position -
Radial side is lateral side
Ulnar side is medial side

Arterial supply to the Lower Limb:
Common iliac -> external iliac -> common femoral -> femoral -> popliteal behind the knee

Fibular more lateral in the leg
Tibia more medial

Arteries of the neck:
Common carotid artery -> external carotid artery -> internal carotid artery
Carotid refers to the neck

Learn the circle of Willis
A stroke is when one of the arteries in the circle of Willis is blocked

Spot test for anatomy -
Might have a cadaver and they'll ask something like what vessel is this, or what vessel feeds this
