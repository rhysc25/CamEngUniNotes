We are now looking at non-equilibrium, which is what happens when we apply a voltage or a current or shine light on it.

Recap of equilibrium -
N-type - $n_0 = N_C exp(-\frac{(E_c - E_F)}{kT})$
Where $N_c = 2 (\frac{2\pi m_n^* kT}{h^2})^{3/2}$
In intrinsic semiconductor, $n_0 = p_0 = n_i$
Law of mass action - $n_0p_0 = n_i^2$

$n_i^2 = N_CN_V exp(\frac{-E_g}{kT})$
Looking at this equation, if we change temperature, we also have to be aware that $N_C$ and $N_V$ change too

Intrinsic Fermi level - $E_{Fi}$

If we dope it, say $n_0 = N_d$, so $p_0 = \frac{n_i^2}{N_d}$

$n_0 = n_i exp(\frac{E_F - E_{Fi}}{kT})$
$p_0 = n_i exp(\frac{E_{Fi}-E_F}{kT})$
When writing in terms of $N_C$ or $N_V$ we're talking about the Fermi level relative to conduction or valence band, whereas when we write them in terms of $n_i$ we are saying relative to $E_{Fi}$

$E_{Fi} - E_{midgap} = \frac{3}{4}kT\ln(\frac{m_p^*}{m_n^*})$
Where $E_{midgap} = \frac{E_C+E_V}{2}$

Transport - movement of carriers
3 phenomena that cause the movement of carriers:
1) Drift - because electric field applied
2) Diffusion 
3) Other

Drift -
If we apply am electric field. If we apply a field the band will bend by $\partial x$. $E_x=\frac{1}{e}\frac{\partial E_C}{\partial x}$
The bands rise in the direction of the field E.

![[Pasted image 20261106192459.png]]
Electrons roll downhill, holes float uphill. This current is called the drift current.

Therefore the drift current for both both point the same direction.

$J_{drift} = \rho v_d$ where $\rho$ is the volume charge 
$\rho$ is volume charge density, which is basically equal to $ep$ (p-type) or $-en$ (n-type)

$J_{p, drift} = (ep)\mu_p E$
$J_{n, drift} = (en)\mu_n E$
Where $\mu = \frac{e\tau}{m^*}$
So total drift current $J_{drift} = e(\mu_p p + \mu_n n)E = \sigma E = \frac{1}{\rho} E$

Depending on p-type or n-type, p or n is much larger, being $N_a$ or $N_d$

Diffusion -
If we have a greater concentration of dopants in one location they will diffuse away.

In diffusion, the currents go in the opposition directions.

$J_{n,diff} = eD_n \frac{\partial n}{\partial c}$, $J_{p,diff} = -eD_p \frac{\partial p}{\partial c}$
Where $D_n$ and $D_p$ are the diffusion coefficients

The total $J_n$ is just $J_{n,drift}+J_{n,diff}$

No matter which semiconductor, if we find $\mu/D$, it always comes out to around 39

This is because of the Einstein relation:
The electrons want to diffuse, but in doing so they're setting up an electric field to prevent it.
This causes an equilibrium, where drift current equals diffusion current.

![[Pasted image 20261106221132.png]]
Graded doping, more doping on the left, so bands bent.

The Fermi level doesn't bend, all the other levels do bend.

You'd expect an electric field to point left, as more negative on the left. But we actually have an effective positive charge on the left as the electrons on the left want to move right by diffusion.

E takes them left, diffusion takes them right.

Potential $= \frac{1}{e}(E_F - E_{Fi})$
$E_x = -\frac{d potential}{dx} = -\frac{1}{e}\frac{d(E_{Fi}(x))}{dx}$

$N_d(x) = n_i exp(\frac{E_F - E_{Fi}(x)}{kT})$
This gets us: $\frac{dE_{Fi}(x)}{dx}= \frac{kT}{N_{d}(x)}\frac{d(N_d(x))}{dx}$
$E_X = \frac{-kT}{e}\frac{1}{N_d(x)}\frac{d(N_d(x))}{dx}$

As $J_{drift}+J_{diff} =0$ we know $e\mu_n N_d(x)E_x + eD_n \frac{d(N_d(x))}{dx}=0$
Subbing in our expression for $E_x$, we get $\mu_n = \frac{eD_n}{kT}$, which shows us where this constant $\mu / D$ comes from
This is the Einstein relation
