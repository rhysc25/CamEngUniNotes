Electron diffraction - Bragg diffraction
Let's consider a beam of X-rays of wavelength $\lambda$ impinging on a regular crystal structure at an angle $\theta$
Constructive interference happens when $n\lambda = 2d\sin\theta$ where $d$ is the lattice spacing

$\psi$ is the wavefunction of the particle
$f(x-vt)$ forward moving
$f(x+vt)$ backward moving

Wavenumber - $k=2\pi / \lambda$

Different types of waves and the form of their $\psi(x,t)$:
Standing wave - $A\cos kx \cos \omega t$ or $A\cos kx \exp^{-j\omega t}$
Travelling wave - $A\cos (kx - \omega t)$ or $A \exp^{j(kx - \omega t)}$
Wavepacket - $\sum_i A_i cos (k_ix - \omega_i t)$ or $\sum_i A_i e^ {j(k_ix - \omega_i t)}$

The Schrodinger equation is a statement of the conservation of energy:
total energy = kinetic energy + potential energy, E = T + V

Total energy from the wavefunction: $E\psi = j\hbar \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t}$
Momentum from the wavefunction: $p\psi = - j\hbar \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}$
Kinetic energy from the wavefunction: $T\psi = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial x^2}$

Time dependent Schrodinger equation:
$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\Delta^2 \psi(r,t)+V(r,t)\psi(r,t)=j\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(r,t)$
Time independent Schrodinger equation:
$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\Delta^2 \psi(r)+V(r)\psi(r)=E\psi(r)$

Postulates of quantum mechanics -
* For a particle in a potential, there is an associated wavefunction which determines everything that can be known about the particle
* The wavefunction satisfies Schrodingers equation at all times
* The wavefunction is a complex, single-valued function and must be continuous, as must its spatial derivatives
* The probability of finding the particle in a volume element $\partial x\partial y\partial z$ about a point $(x,y,z)$ is:
  $P(x,y,z)=|\psi|^2\partial x \partial y \partial z$

Uncertainty principle - To measure the wavelength with infinite accuracy, we would need an infinitely extended wavepacket, which implies the particle could be anywhere
Conversely, if we know exactly where a particle is, then its wavepacket must be infinitely small, so we cannot know its wavelength and hence momentum

If $\Delta x$ and $\Delta p$ are the uncertainties in position and momentum respectively, then $\Delta x \Delta p \geq \frac{\hbar}{2}$
This also implies $\Delta E \Delta t \geq \frac{\hbar}{2}$

If we consider a particle in an infinite potential well, and work it through, we get the solution:
$\psi=B\sin(\frac{\pi n}{L}x)$, where n is the quantum number
From normalisation, we require $\int_0^L |\psi|^2 dx = 1$
We can see we have limited number of states, like the resonant waves on a string, and energy is quantised.
The lowest possible energy state is $n=1$, the ground state

The Hydrogen Atom -
$V(r)=\frac{-e^2}{4\pi \epsilon_0 r}$. To solve the TISE, we will need to put it in polar coordinates
This will have to be solved as a separable differential equation
The phi component solution: $Ae^{jm_l\phi}$, which repeated every $2\pi$
The theta component solution: The set of Legendre functions $P_{l,m_l}$
The radial r component: $\frac{f_{n,l}(r)}{r}exp(\frac{-r}{a})$

For the one electron system, it is the value of $n$ alone which determines the energy of the electron $E_n=\frac{-e^4 m}{8\epsilon_0^2h^2n^2}$

Interpretation -
There are discrete states in which the electron can exist, which are denoted by three quantum numbers: $n,l$ and $m_l$
$l$ and $m_l$ determine the angular momentum of the electron whereas $n$ determines the energy
For simplicity the states are denoted by a number followed by a letter - $1s,2s,2p,...$

Pauli Exclusion Principle - 
In a multi electron system there can never be more than one electron in the same state
Electrons have a fourth quantum number $m_s$, the spin. For electrons: $m_s=\pm 1/2$
It's important for describing electron behaviour in a magnetic field

The magnitude of the spin angular momentum is given by $S=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{1}{2})}\hbar\approx 0.87\hbar$
$m_s$ determines the direction of the spin angular momentum, the component in the z direction. $S_z=m_s\hbar=\pm \frac{1}{2}\hbar$

It is for this reason that not all elements are noble gases, but have preferred numbers of electrons and hence bond together to make molecules
