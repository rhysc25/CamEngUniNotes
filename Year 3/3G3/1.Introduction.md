Central nervous system consists of brain and spinal cord, the rest is the peripheral nervous system

Neuron structure -
* Cell body
* Dendrites - receive messages from other cells
* Axon - passes messages away from the cell body to other neurons, muscles or glands
* Myelin sheath - surrounds axon and helps speed neural impulse
* Terminal branches of axon

Intracellular recording - heat up middle of a 1mm glass tube and pull ends apart at a high velocity, creating a micropipette, then fill with conducting solution to make a tiny electrode
Extracellular recording - place an electrode outside but near the cell body. Can only pick up spikes in $V_m$, and often mixes up signals typically from 2-3 neurons. Because of this, you may want to use a whole bunch of electrodes in an array

Axons contact other neurons at synapses. Boutons (swollen ends of the axon branch) discharge chemical neurotransmitter. They bind to receptors and cause charge to be injected into the other neuron. The charge magnitude depends on "synaptic strength"

Discharge happens only if $V_m$ becomes large enough on the presynaptic side. When does this happen? 
$V_m$ is the netgative potential to the outside, at rest inside the axon. Activity propagates down the axon, leading to stereotypical action potential
Action potential = spike = impulse = firing

What does $\lambda$ depend on?
$\lambda = \sqrt{\frac{Rr_m}{2r_L}}$ where $r_m$ is the specific membrane resistance, inversely proportional to the ion channel density $\rho$ in membrane, and $r_L$ is the longitudinal (intracellular) resistivity

Or $\lambda \propto \sqrt{\frac{R}{\rho}}$. So we could increase cable radius, decrease ion channel density, or not rely entirely on passive propagation of small signals in the first place!

Sensation is receiving inputs from sensors
Perception is interpreting those inputs

Combining evidence and expectations - $p(c|red)\propto p(red|c)p(c)$
Perception is fundamentally a matter of probabilistic inference
The brain computes the posterior distribution

So we need information about the prior and the likelihood function. Both must be learned through experience
Our prediction should be $\hat{x}=\text{argmax}_xp(x|s)$
