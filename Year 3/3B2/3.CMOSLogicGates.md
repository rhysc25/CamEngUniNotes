At every point in time, each gate output is connected to either $V_{DD}$ or $V_{SS}$ via a low resistive path, thus a low outout impedance

The sizing of transistors controls the rise and fall propagation delays, which should be made approximately equal. Traditionally, the PMOS widths are doubled of that of NMOS. This ratio can vary from 1.5 to 2.5

Gates with a fan-in greater than 4 should be avoided, as the propagation time increases as a quadratic in the worst case

CMOS Sequential Logic
Two common examples of bistables -
1) Latches - propagate values from input to outptu continuously
2) Flip flops - propagate values at discrete points in time. Edge-triggered

Semionductor Memory Circuits -
A memory/cell array is an NxM array of one bit storage cells, so the total memory is $2^{N+M}$
All cells in a column share the same I/O line, only one row/word at a time

ROM - 
Data stored is encoded by absence or presence of MOS transistores at row-column intersections.
Upon address selection transistors in correspoinding lines are turned on

SRAM -
Necessary to read out their data and also update or write the data
Basic RAM cell: a pair of cross-coupled inverters

DRAM -
Simpler circuits to reduce area of memory
Memory cells based on a capacitive storage element with significantly reduced component count.
Logic 1 - presence of a small packet of electrical charge stored on a compact capacitance
Leakage currents can remove the stored charge in a matter of a few milliseconds
Need to refresh the logic signal frequently
Best for memories in excess of 256Kbytes

New types of transistor -
As we shrink the L and $t_{ox}$ of transistors, we increase leakage

FinFETs - the gate wraps around a vertical fin on two or three sides, providing superior electrostatic control over the channel
They significantly reduce short-channel effects and have low leakage
There is a limited choice on fin heights (width quantisation)
